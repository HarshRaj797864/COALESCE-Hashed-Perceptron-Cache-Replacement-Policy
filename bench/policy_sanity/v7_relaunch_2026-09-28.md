## Observed, not yet claimed

1. **Arm A beats arm B in 8 of 8 fixed-2 MB configurations**: canneal 4/8/16c
   and barnes 4c, both regimes. At the scaled geometry arm A leads at 4c and
   8c, and arm B at 16c by 0.008%. On single core the A/B was close (B
   +0.13 pp; `coalesce_ab_2026-09-24.md`). No rule was committed for choosing
   between arms after the multi-core results. **Which arm is reported as
   "COALESCE" is the owner's decision.** Arm B is the design as documented:
   hits train the allocating PC, and the rescue keys on the line address.
   Arm A keeps the two inconsistencies that B fixed. Choosing A because it won
   here would be choosing after seeing the evaluation data.
2. **`coalesce_no_sharer` is slower than LRU in the shared regime at 4c and 8c
   at both geometries** (arm A: −3.74% / −1.87% fixed, −8.79% / −2.80%
   scaled).
   - It is **slower than random** at 4c at both geometries and at 8c fixed.
   - Its LLC load hit rate is below random's at 4/8/16c fixed (15.9 / 16.0 /
     15.9% against 22.3 / 22.8 / 23.2%) and at 4c scaled.
   - At 8c scaled it is ahead of random on both counts.

   A replacement policy worse than random suggests a pathology, not a missing
   feature. The likeliest is ties going to the first way: 30–50% of evictions
   tie, and COALESCE has no tie-breaker (known, recorded, frozen). **Not
   quotable as the sharer feature's value** until that is measured.
3. **At the scaled geometry COALESCE takes more total LLC misses than LRU**
   while matching or beating it on loads. At 8c shared, arm A has 620,126
   misses against LRU's 548,498, with a load hit rate of 88.6% against 86.8%.
   So the extra misses are non-load: writebacks and translations. This is
   consistent with A4's finding that the `+40` MODIFIED bias costs. An input to
   the post-B3 policy work.

## Correction to `v7_crossregime_2026-09-26.md`

That doc proposed the scaled geometry, "where the private hit rate stays near
50%", as the cross-regime experiment **without** the pressure confound.
**Wrong.** It mixed two metrics:
- the confound was stated in **LLC load** hit rate (1.69 / 0.64 / 0.34% private
  vs ~24% shared);
- the "~50%" is the **total** hit rate from `llc_sizing_2026-09-19.md`, where
  writebacks hit ~100%.

On loads, the two geometries compare as follows:

| LRU LLC load hit rate | 4c | 8c | 16c |
|---|---|---|---|
| fixed 2 MB, private | 1.69% | 0.64% | 0.34% |
| fixed 2 MB, shared | 23.65% | 23.95% | 24.80% |
| scaled, private | 26.05% | 26.30% | 26.41% |
| scaled, shared | 67.65% | 86.81% | 97.74% |

The scaled geometry **widens** the pressure gap between the regimes, from
22–24 pp to 42–71 pp. Aliasing shrinks the shared working set at any cache
size, so the pressure difference is itself an effect of sharing, and no single
geometry removes it.

**What does match** is fixed-2 MB shared against scaled private: the same
traces and core count, sharing on vs off, with LRU load hit rates within
1.6–2.4 pp at every core count. That pairing is the closest to a
pressure-matched cross-regime comparison the existing trees allow.

Its caveat: the LLCs differ in size (2 MB against 8–32 MB, with more sets), and
set count alone moves some policies. Hawkeye's sampled-set count, for one,
varies with geometry (PLAN § 8).

The scaled-private jobs now running are still needed, **for this pairing**,
not for the reason given on 2026-09-26.

## Internals — added 2026-09-29

Source: `internals_v7.txt`, `check_policy_internals.sh` over every tree. It
has 90 COALESCE-family arm rows, including the scaled-private arm jobs that
had finished by then.

- **Every counter is non-zero in every arm row** except barnes 4c scaled
  shared (both arms, all three variants). That configuration takes no LLC
  miss (above), so nothing is ever evicted; hit-trains are non-zero there
  (23,412).
- **`detrains == ghost_inserts` in every row, by construction.** Both count
  each sampled eviction, and `train()` applies the confidence threshold
  inside. So the counter shows the path runs; it does not show that weights
  moved.
- **Rescue rate (ghost_rescues / ghost_inserts), `coalesce`, canneal:**

| | 4c private | 8c private | 16c private | 4c shared | 8c shared | 16c shared |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pre-fix | | 11.49% | | | | |
| arm A | 8.30% | 3.14% | 0.56% | 27.87% | 26.04% | 25.21% |
| arm B | 33.78% | 21.20% | 9.58% | 52.89% | 48.87% | 44.72% |

**Arm B's rate is above the pre-fix rate, and that is expected.** The V7 item
"rescue rate well below the pre-fix floor" fits arm A only. The two arms key
the ghost differently:
- **Arm A** keys the slot on (tag, PC). A lookup at fill time uses the
  *incoming* PC, so a line is rescued only if the same PC brings it back.
  On `429.mcf` that missed 70.5% of genuine early evictions (`8de8c42`).
- **Arm B** keys slot and Bloom filter on the tag alone, the address bits
  above the set index. Its entries compare 14 of those real bits, so a false
  match needs a 1-in-16,384 partial-tag collision.
- **Pre-fix**, the partial tag held only 3 real bits and the slot depended on
  the PC alone, so its rate was same-PC-restricted *and* inflated by false
  matches.

Removing the false matches lowers the rate (pre-fix → A). Removing the PC
restriction raises it (A → B). In the shared regime the rates are higher in
both arms, because an evicted shared line is often re-fetched by another core.

**For the A/B decision:** arm A's multi-core lead (8/8 fixed-2 MB
configurations) comes with a rescue that ignores most genuine early
evictions. Whether that helps by accident, or whether B's much larger rescue
volume over-trains (5× boost per rescue), is a question for the post-B3 work.
It is not a reason to call A the fixed policy.
