# A4 — single-core baseline validation, complete

> **Measured on the pre-fix policy** (added 2026-09-23). COALESCE's ghost-tag
> rescue could not tell one address from another until `97aa5da`; every
> COALESCE figure below predates that fix and is provisional until re-run. See
> `coalesce_ghost_defect_2026-09-23.md`, "Which COALESCE findings are now
> provisional".


**2026-09-21.** 132/132 runs: 11 policies x 12 LLC-sensitive SPEC CPU2006
SimPoints, single core, 50M warmup + 50M simulation, 2 MB 16-way LLC,
ChampSim @ develop f9ed890e.

This is what both HiPC reviewers asked for and what the filed rebuttal promised
for the camera-ready. The Sept rebuttal validated on **one** usable trace: the
other, 600.perlbench, returned byte-identical results for all four policies
because it never touches the LLC.

## The two checks that matter: both pass

**Every policy behaves distinctly.** No two policies share a cycle count on any
discriminating trace. Hawkeye was previously byte-identical to SRRIP -- same
IPC to four figures, same cycle count -- while passing every structural guard.
Across 10 traces, that class of defect is now excluded.

**Ordering matches the literature.** SHiP < Hawkeye < Mockingjay, as Table I of
Mockingjay (HPCA'22) reports.

## On the magnitudes

They run about twice the published figures, and that is expected rather than
alarming. These twelve traces were **chosen** for LLC sensitivity, and the two
that failed to discriminate were then dropped. The published geomeans cover
SPEC2006 + SPEC2017 + GAP including many benchmarks where every policy ties,
which pulls their averages toward zero. An exact match against a differently
composed set would be more suspicious than a mismatch.

**Why the inflation is uneven -- resolved, see `a4_inflation_2026-09-21.md`.**
Hawkeye 2.13x and Mockingjay 1.98x against SHiP's 1.25x needed an explanation
that enrichment alone does not give. The guess recorded here first -- that
reuse-predicting policies gain more from LLC-*pressured* workloads -- is wrong,
and the data refutes it: SHiP's high-pressure half is 0.99x published while its
low-pressure half is the inflated one, and the correlation with pressure is
*steepest* for the least-inflated policy.

The axis is reuse, not pressure. The Hawkeye-minus-SHiP gap tracks LRU's load
hit rate at rho -0.74 (leave-one-out -0.85 .. -0.64) against only +0.37 for
MPKI. Half this subset -- libquantum, lbm and bwaves at 0.0% LRU hit rate, mcf
at 1.3% -- is streaming or thrashing, where nearly every line is dead on
arrival. SHiP's signature scheme has little to learn when the answer is "all of
them"; OPT reconstruction can still protect the minority of live lines.
Selecting for LLC sensitivity selected, in practice, for that class.

## COALESCE: both ablations beat the full policy

    coalesce_no_sharer  +2.73%
    coalesce_no_mesi    +2.49%
    coalesce            +2.23%

On single-core SPEC there is no coherence. `sharer_mask` is always 1, so the
`sharers >= 2` bias never fires. But **every dirty line is MODIFIED**, so the
`+40` MODIFIED bias fires constantly.

So in this setting that bias is not a coherence signal at all -- it is a
dirty-bit preference, and it costs 0.26 percentage points against simply
removing it. The feature is doing harm where it carries no information.

This does not speak to the multi-core case, which is what the policy exists
for. It does say the MODIFIED bias should be gated on there being more than one
sharer, or on the regime, rather than applying unconditionally.

## Caveat

Single core, so every coherence feature COALESCE depends on is inert by
construction. Its mid-pack placement here is expected and is not evidence about
the multi-core claim. What this run establishes is that the **baselines** are
sound -- which is precisely what the reviewers disputed.
